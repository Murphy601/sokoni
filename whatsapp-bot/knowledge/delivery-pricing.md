# Delivery pricing (who sets what)

Two different things are called "delivery", and they are priced by different
people. Getting this wrong is the most common mistake when answering a seller.

## Local rider deliveries — Sokoni sets the price

Both ends inside the Nairobi metro (Nairobi, Kiambu, and the Machakos metro
towns such as Syokimau, Athi River, Kitengela). A Sokoni rider carries it.

The platform prices these by distance. The seller's own rates do not apply.

- **KES 400** minimum, covering the first 5 road km
- **KES 25** per road km after that
- **KES 1,500** ceiling, however far

Straight-line distance is scaled by 1.3, because a rider follows roads.
Measured from the seller's pickup pin to the buyer's delivery pin.

A seller cannot change these. A seller who says "but I charge 200 for
Nairobi" is describing their upcountry rates, which still apply upcountry.

## Upcountry — the seller sets the price

Anywhere outside the metro. No rider is involved; the seller ships by courier
or parcel sacco with a waybill. Their Seller Hub county tiers set the price,
exactly as before, and Sokoni does not touch them.

## Never quote a figure for a specific order

The fee depends on the distance between two pins, so it cannot be worked out
in conversation. Say how it is calculated and that the exact figure appears at
checkout before anyone pays. Do not estimate, and do not read a number off an
old order and assume it still applies.

## Why a delivery might come out at the minimum

If either pin is missing the fee falls back to KES 400 rather than blocking
the order. That is usually a seller who has not set a pickup pin. The answer
is to set one, not to argue about the fee.

## Where the money goes

The buyer pays the delivery fee into escrow with the item. After the rider
confirms delivery, Sokoni takes a 10% platform cut, M-Pesa takes its B2C
charge, and the rest goes to the rider. On a KES 400 delivery the rider nets
KES 345.
